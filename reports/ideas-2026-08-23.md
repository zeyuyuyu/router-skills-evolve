## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. arxiv:2608.09805 — Perturbed Parameter Policy Optimization (3PO)
**Submitted:** August 2026

**Core claim:** Standard GRPO over-relies on action-space rollout diversity; when all K
rollouts for a prompt receive the same reward (zero-variance group), no gradient flows.
3PO draws K independent parameter-space perturbations of the policy (each slightly
different θ + ε_k) and samples one rollout per perturbation, guaranteeing inter-rollout
diversity at the parameter level rather than hoping for it at the output level.

**Method:** For each training prompt, sample K Gaussian perturbations of θ; use standard
GRPO advantage across these K rollouts; update with PPO-style clipping. No temperature
tricks. Consistently fewer zero-advantage groups than GRPO, DAPO, and DR-GRPO on HumanEval
and MATH500.

**Relevance to MERA:** ACR=52.4% zero-variance groups is MERA's central Phase 3b bottleneck
(confirmed across EXP-176 through EXP-186). This is a complementary approach to EXP-184
(latent credit) and EXP-186 (CVPO variance scaling): parameter-space diversity, not
output-space resampling. Possible to implement in ~50 lines as a patch to
`src/pipeline/grpo_train_simple.py`. **Should be next GPU slot after EXP-186.**

### 2. arxiv:2509.21880 — RL-ZVP: No Prompt Left Behind
**Submitted:** September 2025 (surfaced in August 2026 GRPO review cluster)

**Core claim:** GRPO's training loop actively discards zero-variance prompts (easy tasks
where all K rollouts are correct, and hard tasks where none is). RL-ZVP treats zero-variance
prompts differently by class: for all-correct prompts, apply entropy regularisation to
prevent exploitation collapse; for all-wrong prompts, fallback to SFT-warmup on the best
rollout by heuristic similarity. Combined with standard GRPO advantage for mixed-outcome
prompts.

**Method:** Three-pathway advantage router: compute ACR → route to (1) entropy penalty, (2)
RL-ZVP SFT-warmup, or (3) standard GRPO advantage. No change to the core GRPO optimizer.
Showed +3.2 pp pass@1 on HumanEval vs. GRPO with same token budget.

**Relevance to MERA:** Directly maps to our ACR=52.4% problem. The three-pathway router is
simple enough to implement. Note: EXP-186 (CVPO) addresses variance-scaling; RL-ZVP
addresses the routing-to-loss step. These are orthogonal and composable.

### 3. arxiv:2605.28577 — Continual Model Routing in Evolving Model Hubs
**Submitted:** May 2026

**Core claim:** When the model hub (set of available small/large models) evolves across
time (new checkpoints, retired models), static routers degrade monotonically. Proposes
Continual Router (CR): a light lightweight incremental learner that updates routing
decisions after each hub change using only the new checkpoint's performance delta on a
held-out calibration set, without retraining from scratch.

**Method:** Logistic routing head initialized from previous checkpoint's weights, trained for
one epoch on calibration pairs (prompt, cost_delta, pass_delta) against the new checkpoint.
Demonstrates graceful routing accuracy degradation: <2% drop per hub update vs. 10-15%
for static routers.

**Relevance to MERA:** Our `train_router_simple.py` retrains the router from scratch each
cycle. Across 4 cycles, this is equivalent to 4 hub updates. If we instead warm-start
the router head from the previous cycle's weights and train for 1 epoch, we might get
faster convergence and better generalization — especially helpful in Cycle 0→1 where the
router dataset is smallest.
