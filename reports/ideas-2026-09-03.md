## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. arxiv:2507.05386 — Reinforcement Fine-Tuning Naturally Mitigates Forgetting in Continual Post-Training
**Submitted:** July 2026

**Core claim:** Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) reduces catastrophic forgetting in
continual post-training even without explicit anti-forgetting mechanisms. Crucially,
the authors show the mitigation is NOT attributable to KL regularization or chain-of-thought
reasoning — the mechanism is subtler, likely tied to the reward-signal sparsity preserving
more of the pretrained parameter geometry than dense supervised gradients.

**Key design axes vs. MERA:**

| Axis | arxiv:2507.05386 | MERA |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-forgetting mechanism | RFT inherent property (not KL, not CoT) | SFT→GRPO checkpoint reinitialization (Decision #6) |
| Continual post-training structure | Single long RL episode | N alternating SFT+GRPO cycles |
| Forgetting evidence | No forgetting spike in RFT vs. SFT | No RL-induced forgetting; cycle-1 SFT dip (Hypothesis F) |
| Distillation component | None (pure RL) | SkillBook procedure prefix + SFT from oracle traces |

**Relevance to MERA:** EXP-203 (queued 2026-08-31) already addresses §7 Remedies using
arxiv:2607.04364 (RL Forgets!). But arxiv:2507.05386 provides a stronger positive claim:
RFT *naturally* prevents forgetting without KL. MERA's SFT→GRPO alternation adds an
even stronger guarantee by restarting GRPO from the latest SFT checkpoint — combining the
"natural RFT anti-forgetting" with an explicit checkpoint boundary. This paper allows §7
to make a stronger claim: MERA doesn't just mitigate forgetting via KL (as in baseline
GRPO), it eliminates the RL-forgetting risk entirely by never accumulating RL gradients
across cycles. The 4-cycle trajectory data (no RL forgetting spikes) is direct evidence.

### 2. arxiv:2607.01763 — Denser ≠ Better: Limits of On-Policy Self-Distillation for Continual Post-Training
**Submitted:** July 2026

**Core claim:** More frequent / denser on-policy self-distillation signals do not
monotonically improve performance in continual post-training. The authors identify a
"density collapse" regime where the student model is updated too frequently against its
own improving teacher, leading to a kind of self-fulfilling loop that degrades
generalization. The key factor is the signal-to-noise ratio of the distillation target,
not just the frequency.

**Relevance to MERA:** MERA uses `SCALING_FORCE_BOTH=1` (the run-both oracle — large
model evaluated on *every* task, not just those the small model fails). CLAUDE.md
Gotchas section explicitly states this "markedly improves the skills/SFT arms." The
"denser ≠ better" finding could appear to challenge this claim. However, MERA's
distillation is fundamentally different from the self-distillation setting studied in
arxiv:2607.01763:

| Axis | arxiv:2607.01763 | MERA |
|---|---|---|
| Distillation source | Same model (self-distillation, student is teacher) | Separate large model (GPT-5.5, genuinely stronger) |
| Signal density | Logit-level, continuous, high frequency | Discrete procedure text (SkillBook), one per cycle |
| Oracle type | None — teacher = current student | Run-both oracle: teacher always solved tasks independently |
| Risk of "density collapse" | High (self-fulfilling loop) | Low (teacher is strictly stronger, different model) |

This distinction is exactly the kind of nuanced point ICLR 2027 reviewers will probe,
especially given MERA's claim that SCALING_FORCE_BOTH=1 "markedly improves" outcomes.
EXP-207 produces the §2b differentiation paragraph that preempts this challenge.

### 3. arxiv:2608.03796 — Efficient Knowledge Distillation for LLMs: Offline Top-K Logits and a Fused Chunked KL Loss
**Submitted:** August 2026

**Core claim:** Offline KD (caching the teacher's top-K logits once and training the
student against the cache) matches online distillation quality while running 29% faster
per iteration and achieving up to 41% higher throughput. The student never needs the
teacher in memory simultaneously.

**Relevance to MERA:** MERA's trace collection phase already uses an offline pattern —
`collect_traces.py` generates teacher traces, saves them as `traces.jsonl`, and the
SFT phase (`traces_to_sft.py` → `train_small_model.py`) trains from the cached traces.
This is effectively offline KD at the sequence level (not logit level). arxiv:2608.03796
is a 2026 citation that validates MERA's trace-caching architecture as an efficient
alternative to online distillation, without requiring any implementation changes.
Flagged as a background citation; not queued as a standalone experiment today (already
covered by the run-both oracle experiments in queue).
