## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. arxiv:2607.07847 — When Does Continual Learning Require Learning
**Submitted:** July 8, 2026 (UC Berkeley + collaborators)

**Core claim:** LLM continual learning is not merely about forgetting mitigation — it is
fundamentally about increasing model competence as the world changes, along two axes:
*space* (new domains arrive over time) and *time* (facts drift under a fixed task).
The paper introduces a mechanism-agnostic benchmark comparing GRPO, SFT, SDFT, SDPO,
prompt-based methods (GEPA, ACE), and context compression (Cartridges, In-place TTT).

**Key design axes vs. MERA:**

| Axis | arxiv:2607.07847 | MERA |
|---|---|---|
| Distillation component | None (all methods self-contained) | SkillBook procedure distillation → procedure prefix |
| Routing component | None (all methods applied to all tasks) | Learned binary router, retrained each cycle |
| Training structure | Single-episode or short-horizon CL | N-cycle SFT→GRPO alternation with checkpoint boundary |
| Teacher traces | In-domain / self-supervised | Oracle traces from GPT-5.5 (run-both oracle) |

**Relevance to MERA:** A naive ICLR 2027 reviewer may classify MERA as "SFT+GRPO
interleaved — already in Table 2 of 2607.07847." The paper is comprehensive enough
that this is a credible challenge. EXP-208 formally documents the four structural
axes absent from the benchmark, producing the §2 positioning paragraph that preempts
the conflation. Key point: MERA's SkillBook distillation and learned router are
co-evolution axes absent from every method in the 2607.07847 protocol.

### 2. arxiv:2603.22455 — SkillRouter: Skill Routing for LLM Agents at Scale
**Submitted:** March 2026

**Core claim:** SkillRouter learns to route LLM agent requests to the appropriate
skill (tool, sub-agent, or capability module) at inference time, optimizing capability
coverage and latency across large skill libraries. Routing is intra-model: SkillRouter
selects *what to do* (which capability), not *which model to call*.

**Key design axes vs. MERA:**

| Axis | SkillRouter (2603.22455) | MERA |
|---|---|---|
| Routing target | Which skill/capability to invoke | Which model (small vs. large) to invoke |
| Routing scope | Intra-model (same model throughout) | Inter-model (different cost/quality tradeoffs) |
| Training signal | Skill-invocation success/failure | Oracle labels: did small model pass the task? |
| Continual update | Static or lightly updated | Retrained from scratch every cycle |

**Relevance to MERA:** An ICLR 2027 reviewer familiar with SkillRouter could conflate
MERA's "SkillBook + router" pipeline with skill-selection routing, especially since both
use the word "skill." EXP-209 produces the §4 footnote (~80 words) and 2-row table that
clarify the orthogonal nature of the two problems: MERA routes to model scale
(a cost/quality tradeoff decision), not to a capability (a functional decomposition
decision). The distinction protects MERA's §4 Router contribution claim at ICLR 2027.

### 3. arxiv:2605.07725 — SOD: Step-wise On-policy Distillation for Small Language Model Agents
**Submitted:** May 2026

**Core claim:** Step-wise on-policy distillation (SOD) provides supervision at each
reasoning step rather than at the trajectory level, improving small-model agent
performance over sequence-level distillation. The per-step teacher signal is on-policy
(teacher conditions on the student's current context).

**Relevance to MERA:** MERA's procedure format (`f"{procedure}\n\n---\n\n{problem}"`)
applies the distilled procedure at the *beginning* of the prompt (sequence level), not
step by step. SOD's step-level supervision is fundamentally different — and more
expensive (requires teacher at every step). This paper is a background citation that
contextualizes MERA's design choice as efficient sequence-level distillation vs.
step-level. Not queued as a standalone experiment today (already saturated by EXP-202's
prefix-suffix teachability audit covering this framing).
