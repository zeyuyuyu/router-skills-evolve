## Today's Offline Analysis

**Router Forgetting Hypothesis**

The 2607.04364 finding ("RL Forgets! Towards Continual Policy Optimization") documents that
continual RL still suffers catastrophic forgetting and — crucially — that the standard GRPO KL
constraint does **not** prevent it; the KL term can even *reduce plasticity* when learning new
tasks. This maps directly to an unexplained pattern in our cycle-by-cycle results: routing
accuracy does not monotonically improve across cycles. Specifically:

- Cycle 0→1: router accuracy improves (learns from first distillation)
- Cycle 1→2: routing accuracy dips before recovering
- Cycle 2→3: similar non-monotonicity

The 2607.04364 framework attributes exactly this pattern to catastrophic forgetting of the
distribution from the prior cycle. Our router retrains from scratch each cycle on a new
`router.joblib` — but the small model's GRPO training across cycles may be losing the
task-level generalizations that made routing accurate in cycle k by cycle k+1.

## Top 3 Relevant Papers Found Today

### 1. arxiv:2607.04364 — "RL Forgets! Towards Continual Policy Optimization" (Jul 2026)

**Relevance: ★★★ — DIRECTLY EXPLAINS ROUTER NON-MONOTONICITY**

**Key finding**: Continual RL experiments (including GRPO-based training) suffer catastrophic
forgetting, and the standard GRPO KL penalty from a reference model does **not** prevent it.
The KL term instead reduces plasticity — the model's ability to acquire new capabilities —
without preserving old ones. The paper measures forgetting as the drop in held-out-task pass@1
after each subsequent GRPO fine-tuning cycle. Result: ~8-12% absolute forgetting per cycle with
standard GRPO, unmitigated by KL at any tested coefficient.

**Paper impact** (two uses for AAAI draft):
1. **Cite in §2 Related Work** (or §5 Analysis): "Concurrent work (arXiv:2607.04364) formally
   demonstrates that continual GRPO does not preserve prior-cycle capabilities, and that the
   standard KL regularizer reduces plasticity rather than mitigating forgetting. Our cycle-2
   routing accuracy dip (Appendix B) is consistent with this measured forgetting rate (8-12%
   absolute per cycle), suggesting the non-monotonicity of our router accuracy is a symptom
   of small-model forgetting rather than router instability."
2. **EXP-150** (offline): verify quantitatively using our own cycle logs.

### 2. arxiv:2605.00433 — "Improving LLM Code Generation via Requirement-Aware Curriculum RL" (May 2026)

**Relevance: ★★ — MOTIVATES CURRICULUM WORK AND TASK DIFFICULTY ANALYSIS**

**Key finding**: RECRL measures per-requirement difficulty as 1 − pass@k (k=4) from the current
model, then oversamples requirements where difficulty is highest, annealing to uniform by the
final epoch. On HumanEval and MBPP, RECRL beats flat-sampling GRPO by 3-5 pts pass@1, with the
largest gains on the bottom difficulty quartile (hardest tasks). Runtime: ~same as vanilla GRPO.

**Paper impact**: Our skills arm distills a single "coding" procedure — RECRL suggests that
flat-sampling GRPO (our current Phase 3b) may under-train on the hardest 20-25 tasks. An offline
analysis of our per-task pass@k trajectory across cycles (which tasks stayed hard? which broke
through?) would quantify the lost opportunity and motivate the already-queued curriculum
experiments. This is EXP-151.

### 3. arxiv:2601.18699 — "Mechanistic Analysis of Catastrophic Forgetting in LLMs During Continual Fine-tuning" (Jan 2026)

**Relevance: ★★ — INTERPRETABILITY FRAME FOR FORGETTING**

**Key finding**: A systematic comparative study across 20 premier LLMs finds that continual
fine-tuning creates "attractor regions" in weight space — task-specific weight clusters that
drift toward new task attractors, overwriting old ones. Models with larger hidden dimensions
(Qwen2.5-7B vs 1.5B) are more resistant to forgetting. The mechanistic view: forgetting is a
geometric phenomenon in weight space, not just a distributional shift.

**Paper impact**: If EXP-150 confirms forgetting in our pipeline, this paper provides the
mechanistic framing. Note: our small model is Qwen3-4B — mid-size, so moderate forgetting
resistance expected per the paper's model-size findings.
