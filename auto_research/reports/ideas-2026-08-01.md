## Top 3 Relevant Papers Found Today

### 1. arxiv:2607.28076 — "Group-Reflective Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning"

**Relevance: ★★★ — HIGHEST PRIORITY TODAY**

**Key finding**: GRSD (Zheng et al., July 30 2026) proposes deriving *capability-aligned* and *outcome-discriminative* guidance directly from the policy's own verified GRPO rollout groups. For each group of K rollouts on the same prompt, successful and failed trajectories are contrasted via a stop-gradient snapshot of the policy itself to produce natural-language "group skills" — reusable decision rules that explain why successful rollouts differed from failed ones. These group skills are then distilled back into the policy as an auxiliary token-level loss alongside the standard GRPO advantage update.

**Key result**: On WebArena and OSWorld, GRSD improves +4.1pp and +3.9pp over vanilla GRPO respectively, with the skill extraction adding <15% wall-clock overhead per batch.

**Direct application**: Our SkillBook currently distills one skill update per *cycle* from GPT-5.5 teacher traces, with extraction happening between cycles (Phase 2). GRSD suggests an intra-cycle analog: extract "rollout-differential skills" from the GRPO training batches themselves, then append to the SkillBook before the *next* cycle's trace collection. This addresses a gap in our current design — the SkillBook sees teacher-curated reasoning patterns but never sees the small model's own failure modes as they evolve during GRPO training.

**Paper impact**: Directly extends §3.3 (SkillBook design) and §2 (Related Work → On-Policy Distillation). If EXP-144 recovers or improves the cycle-1 skills arm, it adds a "intra-cycle skill refinement" row to Table 9, which currently only shows inter-cycle skill updates.

### 2. arxiv:2605.28860 — "Mechanistic Origins of Catastrophic Forgetting: Why RL Preserves Circuits Better than SFT?"

**Relevance: ★★★ — MECHANISTIC GROUNDING FOR HYPOTHESIS F**

**Key finding** (Rojas Nunez et al., ACL 2026): Using differential circuit vulnerability (DCV) — a head-level measure of attention circuit degradation — the authors compare SFT and RL (Dr.GRPO) on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct. SFT adapts faster to the target task but causes substantially greater disruption to attention head circuits, causing catastrophic forgetting of prior capabilities. RL preserves a larger fraction of the base circuit at the cost of slower task adaptation. The mechanism: SFT's dense gradient signal (all tokens, all positions) disrupts multi-head attention patterns that implement prior task circuits; RL's sparse gradient (advantage-weighted positions only) leaves most circuits intact.

**Direct application**: Our cycle-1 phenomena map exactly onto this framework:
- SFT epoch-1 (loss 0.178): moderate circuit adaptation → skills arm 65.85% maintained from cycle-0
- SFT epoch-2 (loss 0.271 — overfit reversal): dense extra-gradient step causes further circuit disruption beyond what the task requires → the 4.88pp skills arm dip is circuit forgetting, not overfitting in the training-loss sense
- GRPO-1: sparse advantage-weighted gradient → partial circuit recovery → explains why cycle-2 (73.17%) recovers without re-running SFT epoch-2

**Paper impact**: §5.4 Hypothesis F currently has three proposed fix paths (EXP-137/138/140). EXP-145 provides *mechanistic validation* using existing checkpoints (zero new training): load the 4 cycle-1 checkpoint stages, run 82-task HumanEval probes, compute CKA (centered kernel alignment) across decoder layers. If SFT-ep2 shows greater attention-layer CKA drop from cycle-0 baseline than SFT-ep1, and GRPO-1 shows partial recovery, this directly confirms the mechanistic story in arxiv:2605.28860 within our own system.

### 3. arxiv:2607.14777 — "SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning"

**Relevance: ★★ — RELATED WORK / POST-AAAI CONSIDERATION**

**Key finding** (July 14 2026): SEED fine-tunes the policy to analyze its completed trajectories and generate natural-language skills (reusable workflows, failure-avoidance rules) within the GRPO training loop. The policy simultaneously acts as the agent, the trajectory analyzer, and the skill library. SEED's "self-evolving" aspect is that skill extraction improves as the policy improves, creating a positive feedback loop.

**Distinction from our approach**: Our pipeline uses a *fixed large model* (GPT-5.5) as the skill extractor between cycles, separating the actor from the analyzer. SEED collapses actor and analyzer into the same (evolving) model. This is more efficient but risks the small model generating low-quality skills early in training when its reasoning is weakest. Our design avoids this by using the large model as the skill analyzer throughout — a defensible choice to highlight as a design decision in §3.3.

**Action**: Not queued today (at 2-experiment cap). Record for §2 Related Work paragraph: "Concurrent with this work, SEED (arXiv:2607.14777) proposes self-distillation within the GRPO loop using the policy as its own skill extractor. MERA differs in using a fixed large-model analyzer to guarantee skill quality early in training when the small model's reasoning is unreliable."

### EXP-144: GRSD-Inspired Intra-Cycle Skill Reflection from GRPO Rollout Groups *(~2.5h GPU)*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| ID | `exp_2026_08_01_001_grsd_intra_cycle_skill_reflection_from_grpo_rollouts` |
| Kind | `grpo_continual` |
| Priority | 6 |
| GPU | ~2.5h (adds ~30min to standard `grpo_continual`) |
| arxiv | 2607.28076 |
| AAAI priority | EXPLORATORY — post-GPU-window; run if A800 restores post-AAAI |
| Dependency | Requires `SCALING_FORCE_BOTH=1` traces with both `large_pass` and `small_pass` |

**Rationale**: arxiv:2607.28076 (GRSD, Jul 30 2026) shows that contrasting successful vs failed GRPO rollouts yields richer skill supervision than trajectory-level outcomes alone (+4.1pp on WebArena). Our SkillBook distills skills from GPT-5.5 teacher traces between cycles (inter-cycle, Phase 2). EXP-144 adds a post-GRPO intra-cycle skill refinement step: after GRPO training completes for cycle N, extract 3-5 "rollout-differential skills" by prompting the large model to contrast successful vs failed rollout pairs from the GRPO training log. Append these micro-skills to the SkillBook before cycle N+1 trace collection. Tests whether intra-cycle skill refinement (from small model's own failure modes) complements inter-cycle distillation (from teacher traces).

**Implementation**: ~20 lines hooking into the end of `grpo_train_simple.py`: (1) load the GRPO training log's per-prompt pass/fail outcomes, (2) sample 5 contrast pairs, (3) call large model with the SkillBook's existing `update_skill` prompt template, (4) append results to `skillbook.json`. No change to the GRPO algorithm or reward signal.

**Expected mechanism**: Small model's failure modes during GRPO training are more fine-grained than those in the teacher trace corpus (which represents GPT-5.5's strategies). Intra-cycle skills capture "where the small model goes wrong *now*" rather than "where a strong model would go well." This addresses a systematic gap in the current SkillBook design.
