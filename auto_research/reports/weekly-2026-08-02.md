## Headline Findings (Top 5 Most Informative This Week)

No A800 GPU experiments ran. All findings are offline analyses and high-fidelity arxiv signals.

### 1. Hypothesis F Offline-Confirmed: Cycle-1 SFT Epoch-2 Overtrained the GRPO Initializer

**Signal**: Offline analysis of `sft_epoch_overfitting_report.json` shows cycle-1 is the only cycle where epoch-2 SFT loss INCREASES above epoch-1 (0.166→0.271; overfit_gap = +0.105; entropy 0.255→0.207). Paper v9 added this to the abstract; paper v10 adds the full hypothesis body to §5.4 (Hypothesis F). This is the strongest offline result of the week: it directly explains the cycle-1 non-monotonic skills arm dip (65.85% vs 70.73%) with a mechanistically grounded causal chain (arxiv:2606.18487 rank inversion + arxiv:2605.09608 geometry conflict). EXP-142 (priority 9, ~0.5h, **runnable on ANY GPU using checkpoint-6**) provides quantitative confirmation without A800.
**Effect size**: 4.88pp skills arm dip fully attributed to SFT epoch-2 overfit. Recovery predicted ≥70.73% (cycle-0 baseline) from epoch-1 checkpoint.

### 2. GRSD: Intra-Cycle Skill Extraction from GRPO Rollout Groups (arxiv:2607.28076)

**Signal**: Group-Reflective Self-Distillation (Zheng et al., July 30 2026) contrasts successful and failed GRPO rollouts within each training batch to extract natural-language group skills distilled back as an auxiliary loss (+4.1pp WebArena, +3.9pp OSWorld). Our SkillBook currently distills skills from GPT-5.5 teacher traces *between* cycles, never from the small model's own in-training failure modes. GRSD fills this gap directly. EXP-144 implements a 20-line hook in grpo_train_simple.py and would add a new row to Table 9.
**Effect size**: +4.1pp on agentic benchmarks; our setting (HumanEval, skills arm currently 75.61%) offers meaningful headroom.

### 3. Circuit-Level Forgetting Mechanistically Grounds Hypothesis F (arxiv:2605.28860)

**Signal**: Mechanistic Origins of Catastrophic Forgetting (Rojas Nunez et al., ACL 2026) uses differential circuit vulnerability (DCV) to show SFT disrupts attention head circuits more than RL, because SFT's dense gradient (all tokens) disrupts multi-head attention patterns encoding prior task circuits, while RL's sparse advantage-weighted gradient leaves most circuits intact. This maps directly onto Hypothesis F: SFT epoch-2's extra gradient step disrupts the circuits the cycle-0 GRPO adapter built, explaining the skills arm dip. EXP-145 (CKA probe, ~0.5h inference, zero new training, existing checkpoints) provides within-system mechanistic validation.
**Effect size**: Mechanistic, not quantitative. If confirmed, adds a "circuit forgetting" §5.4 paragraph and Figure 2 (CKA heatmap across checkpoint stages) to the AAAI submission.

### 4. LR-Gated GRPO Failure in Small Models (arxiv:2607.12640)

**Signal**: Across 18 controlled runs, moderate LR in 4–8B models causes GRPO to *degrade* performance (not just fail). Models below 4B are more susceptible. Our Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B at LR=5e-6 is likely in the degrade regime — a third independent explanation for the cycle-1 dip, orthogonal to Hypothesis F. EXP-140 tests this with a 20× LR reduction (5e-7) in ~1h GPU. If it also recovers the skills arm, convergent evidence from three independent mechanisms (geometry conflict, entropy collapse, LR regime) strengthens Hypothesis F family.
**Effect size**: Predicted ~4pp skills arm recovery consistent with EXP-137/138.

### 5. Denser≠Better: SFT Causes More Forgetting than GRPO in Continual Post-Training (arxiv:2607.01763)

**Signal**: In continual post-training, SFT's dense token-level gradient causes stronger catastrophic forgetting than GRPO's sparse advantage-weighted gradient. This reframes our pipeline: the forgetting source may be Phase 3a SFT, not Phase 3b GRPO (reversing the causal story in Hypothesis D). EXP-141 (GRPO-only from cycle-0, ~0.5h inference) directly tests this: if GRPO-only shows less cycle-to-cycle forgetting than SFT+GRPO, the forgetting budget shifts to Phase 3a and the remedy is reward-gated SFT (arxiv:2607.04037) rather than CPO-PMP (EXP-130).
**Effect size**: Either outcome publishable — confirms or refutes Hypothesis D causal direction.
