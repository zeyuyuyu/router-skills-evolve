## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. arxiv:2609.07786 — Signed Rescue Routing: Harm-Aware Cascades for Efficient LLM Inference (Sep 7, 2026)

**Authors**: Zheyuan Wang, Siyu Li, Peiqiao Song, Sijia Chen, Qianqian Song, Qian Liu

**Core idea**: LLM cascade routing should optimize the *signed conditional gain* — P(large correct | small wrong) minus P(large incorrect | small correct) — rather than just escalating when the small model is uncertain. This signed gain is the Bayes-optimal routing score under a fixed escalation budget. The paper separates the beneficial case (large rescues a small-model failure) from the harmful case (large degrades a correct answer) and ranks by their difference.

**MERA intersection**: MERA's oracle label (`label=1` when small fails, `label=0` when small succeeds) approximates the first term P(large correct | small wrong) ≈ 1 for coding tasks where gpt-5.5 rarely regresses. Under the near-zero harm assumption on HumanEval, MERA's oracle label IS a coarser implementation of SRR's signed gain. The key MERA advantage not present in SRR: the router co-evolves across N cycles, re-calibrating what counts as "easy" as the student improves.

**Paper impact**: §2 LLM Routing needs a Sep 2026 citation here; §4.2 can note the zero-harm approximation.

**→ EXP-212 queued**

### 2. arxiv:2609.04172 — Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models II: One Training Example (Sep 4, 2026)

**Core idea**: Trains OPD on a single query at the data-minimal limit. Finds that 1-query OPD reaches 71.5% state coverage (fraction of rollout states visited by full-data training that the 1-query set also visits) and recovers most of full-data OPD's validation gain. Adding semantically distinct queries raises coverage monotonically: 16 queries reach 98.9% — near saturation.

**MERA intersection**: MERA's SFT collapse at ~19 hard-task teacher pairs (CLAUDE.md gotcha: nan-grad steps, pass@1 → 0) is directly explained by this paper. The ~19 hard pairs are semantically redundant (all HumanEval tasks where Qwen3-4B fails) — analogous to a single-query regime, not 16 distinct queries. State coverage is therefore near 71% (low), creating sparse gradient signal under gradient accumulation. With SFT_INCLUDE_SUCCESS=1 (~77 pairs including both easy and hard), semantically diverse queries push coverage toward the saturation regime → stable training. This turns a pragmatic fix into a theoretically grounded design decision.

**→ EXP-213 queued (companion: arxiv:2609.05198)**

### 3. arxiv:2609.05198 — What Matters in On-Policy Distillation? A Perspective on Data Efficiency and Data Selection (Sep 4, 2026)

**Authors**: Zhinan Hou, Jiaqi Zhang, Xunliang Cai, Keyou You

**Core idea**: 1-shot OPD is consistently effective; gains are not driven by high token entropy but by longer CoT paths that hard problems naturally produce. Training on longer CoT maintains closer teacher alignment over a long reasoning horizon and teaches reflection patterns absent in short CoTs.

**MERA intersection**: Validates MERA's hard-task selection (hard problems → better distillation signal) but introduces a complication: MERA runs with `enable_thinking=False` on Qwen3 (CLAUDE.md: tau2 corpus has no CoT), so hard-task traces in MERA do NOT have the long CoT paths this paper identifies as the source of improvement. The per-example gain is therefore limited even when state coverage is adequate. This is a forward-looking limitation the paper should acknowledge.

**→ Scope included in EXP-213**
