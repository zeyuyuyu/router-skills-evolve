## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. F-GRPO: Don't Let Your Policy Learn the Obvious and Forget the Rare — arxiv:2602.06717 (February 2026, revised August 2026)
**Relevance: HIGH — directly addresses all-fail group waste in our K=8 GRPO on hard HumanEval problems**

F-GRPO identifies that at finite rollout budgets (K=8), all-fail groups (no correct rollout sampled for a prompt) are common on hard problems. At pass@1 = 0.1, the probability of an all-fail group at K=8 is (0.9)^8 ≈ 43%. These groups produce zero positive gradient, silently wasting training slots and leaving hard problems under-optimized. F-GRPO's fix: extend sampling only for all-fail groups up to K_max=32 at T_explore=1.2, guaranteeing ≥1 correct rollout per prompt when any correct solution exists. Results: F-GRPO at K=8+K_max=32 achieves +3.5%/+11.8% on AIME 2025 at 4× less compute than standard GRPO at K=32.

MERA connection: By cycle 3, Qwen3-4B still fails ~30% of HumanEval hard tail at pass@1. With K=8, roughly 20–43% of training steps on these problems are all-fail groups — no gradient for those prompts. F-GRPO's minority-oracle sampling recovers signal from these wasted steps at marginal cost (easy problems that already hit correct at K=8 incur no extra inference). Distinct from Cue-GRPO (EXP-176, which redistributes credit among already-sampled correct rollouts) — the two are complementary.

### 2. The Dark Room in the Reward Channel — arxiv:2607.21273 (July 2026)
**Relevance: HIGH — identifies a normalization pathology in sparse binary-reward GRPO that may explain cycle-3 training instability**

GRPO normalizes advantages as A_norm = (A − mean) / std per group. In sparse binary-reward settings (pass@1), as the policy masters easy problems, many groups become all-success (std → 0). The normalized advantages remain at ±1 magnitude, generating full-scale gradient pressure from trivially-correct groups that contain zero learning signal. The fix (mean-only normalization) eliminates the pathology. Tested on code generation: mean-only normalization matches or exceeds std-normalized GRPO while reducing training variance at later cycles.

MERA connection: At cycle 3+, Qwen3-4B has mastered ~70% of HumanEval. With K=8, many groups for easy problems are all-success. Std-norm produces noise-gradient from these groups, potentially destabilizing convergence on the hard tail. Mean-only normalization (a 1-line change to `src/pipeline/grpo_train_simple.py`) should reduce noise and improve routing accuracy on the test split. Distinct from all previous experiments (EXP-172/173 addressed forgetting via replay/curriculum; this addresses normalization stability).

### 3. Reward Modeling for RL-Based LLM Reasoning — arxiv:2602.09305 (February 2026)
**Relevance: MEDIUM — survey of reward model design for reasoning; insights captured by EXP-183/184**

Surveys PRM, ORM, and hybrid reward models for RL-based LLM reasoning. Key finding: binary outcome rewards (our pass@1) are robust to reward hacking but have high variance under group normalization; dense PRMs reduce variance but introduce shaping pathologies (see Dark Room above). Our binary pass@1 is well-motivated and this survey reinforces the choice, but it doesn't provide a new experiment angle beyond what F-GRPO and Dark Room already cover. Not queued independently.

### EXP-183 — F-GRPO Finite Rollout Tail-Miss Fix via Minority-Oracle Sampling

**Paper:** arxiv:2602.06717

**Rationale:** Standard GRPO at K=8 produces all-fail groups for hard HumanEval problems at a rate of ~20–43% (depending on problem difficulty). These groups contribute zero positive gradient; the training slot is wasted and hard-problem pass@1 does not improve. For MERA, implement in `src/pipeline/grpo_train_simple.py`: after K initial rollouts, check if any pass the verifier; if not, sample K' more at T_explore=1.2 until ≥1 correct or K_max=32 total. Distinct from Cue-GRPO (EXP-176): Cue-GRPO weights credit among sampled-correct rollouts; F-GRPO ensures hard prompts produce correct rollouts for the group.
