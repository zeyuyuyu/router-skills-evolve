## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. arxiv:2605.07395 — "Unsolvability Ceiling in Multi-LLM Routing: An Empirical Study of Evaluation Artifacts"

**Key finding:** Multi-LLM routing benchmarks systematically overstate gains because a fixed fraction of tasks is "unsolvable" by the small model regardless of the routing decision — the small model fails on them no matter what. The paper introduces the "unsolvability ceiling" metric: the information-theoretic upper bound on routing accuracy given the task hardness distribution. Systems close to the ceiling are near-optimal; systems far below it have room to improve.

**Relevance to MERA:** MERA's ACR (All-Correct Rate among small model rollouts) is 52.4% — implying ~52.4% of HumanEval tasks are all-fail. For these, the correct routing decision is always "send to large" (GPT-5.5 succeeds). If MERA's router correctly escalates all-fail tasks and correctly keeps solvable tasks small-local, the theoretical ceiling is determined by how often these decisions are correct. The 93.04% routing accuracy in §4.3 should be interpreted against this ceiling, not a trivially weak baseline. → **EXP-190**

### 2. arxiv:2606.29296 — "Process Advantage Signal Shaping: A Paradigm-Agnostic Middleware for Process-Supervised RL in LLM Reasoners"

**Key finding:** PASS provides a unified diagnostic taxonomy for GRPO-style RL structural pathologies: (1) *advantage collapse* — all-fail groups produce zero gradient; (2) *advantage masking* — high-variance groups dominate the policy update; (3) *reward clipping* — binary or truncated rewards distort relative group rankings. The paper proposes middleware transformations that normalize the advantage signal before the policy gradient step, applicable to any RL algorithm (GRPO, REINFORCE++, DAPO, PPO).

**Relevance to MERA:** MERA's §2c and §5.3/5.4 currently discuss these pathologies piecemeal via separate citations: CVPO (EXP-186) for (2), RSTG (EXP-150) for (1), OC-GRPO (EXP-148) for sampling fix. PASS provides an umbrella framework that subsumes all three. A camera-ready §2c sentence — "GRPO's structural pathologies (PASS taxonomy [arxiv:2606.29296]) motivate our three-mechanism dark room remedy suite" — consolidates the motivation and adds a new citation not previously in the paper. An offline audit classifies MERA's cycle-1/3 advantage distributions under the PASS taxonomy. → **EXP-191**

### 3. arxiv:2606.10528 — "Representation-Aware Advantage Estimation: Your Reward Model Provides More Than A Scalar Output" (GraphAE)

**Key finding:** GraphAE treats each GRPO rollout group as a graph where nodes = individual responses. Advantages are computed via graph propagation, allowing each sample to incorporate contextual information from neighboring (semantically similar) responses in the group. This enables richer advantage estimates than pure within-group normalization, and provides non-zero signal for groups where most responses fail (partial-credit propagation from the few that succeed).

**Relevance to MERA:** Could complement MERA's zero-advantage all-fail groups by propagating advantage signal cross-task (similar tasks that succeeded provide gradient signal to all-fail tasks).
