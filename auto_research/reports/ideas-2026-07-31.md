## Top 3 Relevant Papers Found Today

### 1. arxiv:2607.12640 — "A Learning-Rate-Gated Failure of GRPO in a Small Language and Vision-Language Model Web Agent"

**Relevance: ★★★ — HIGHEST PRIORITY TODAY**

**Key finding**: Across 18 controlled runs at 4B–8B scale, moderate-to-high LRs cause GRPO to *degrade* performance (not just fail to improve). Two failure regimes emerge: a "degrade regime" at moderate LR (localised parameter drift in attention + MLP blocks) and a "collapse regime" at high LR (diffuse drift). GRPO is only beneficial when there is genuine *headroom* — the sampled policy succeeds more often than the greedy baseline. Otherwise, the policy gradient fires on marginal improvements and damages the representation.

**Direct application**: Our model is Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B — smaller than the 4B lower bound studied in the paper, making it *more* susceptible to the degrade regime at standard learning rates (~1e-5). The cycle-1 skills arm dip (65.85% vs 70.73% cycle-0) has been attributed to SFT epoch-2 plasticity loss (EXP-136/137/138). EXP-140 adds a third orthogonal hypothesis: the standard LR places the 1.5B model in the degrade regime, independently of plasticity.

**Paper impact**: §5.4 Hypothesis F now has three independent fix paths — epoch selection (EXP-137), entropy warmup (EXP-138), LR reduction (EXP-140) — creating a 3-row ablation table. If all three recover the skills arm, the paper can argue convergent evidence for the cycle-1 plasticity/LR sensitivity. If only LR reduction works, the mechanism shifts from "SFT overfitting" to "degrade-regime GRPO," a structurally different finding.

### 2. arxiv:2607.01763 — "Denser ≠ Better: Limits of On-Policy Self-Distillation for Continual Post-Training"

**Relevance: ★★★ — NOVEL FORGETTING ANGLE**

**Key finding**: On-policy self-distillation (SDPO) causes *more* catastrophic forgetting than GRPO in continual post-training. The mechanism: the distillation loss fires on every token regardless of reward signal, inducing large parameter-space drift. GRPO adapts more conservatively — gradients only fire on rollouts that change the reward — preserving prior capabilities better.

**Direct application**: Our SFT phase IS teacher distillation: we minimise cross-entropy on every token of GPT-5.5 teacher traces. Per the paper's prediction, this is the "dense" gradient regime that causes the most forgetting. The observed cycle-1 forgetting (skills arm dip, ACR decline) may originate partly in the SFT phase rather than in GRPO. EXP-141 tests this by skipping the SFT phase and running GRPO-only from the cycle-0 checkpoint.

**Paper impact**: New §5.5 "Role of Teacher Distillation in Forgetting" paragraph. Two possible outcomes:
- **GRPO-only forgets less** (consistent with 2607.01763): supports shortening/removing SFT phase in future cycles, or switching to reward-gated distillation (2607.04037)
- **SFT+GRPO forgets less** (counter to 2607.01763): suggests our offline teacher distillation acts as implicit regularisation; a novel positive finding distinct from the online SDPO regime studied in the paper

Either outcome produces a publishable paragraph grounded in current literature.

### 3. arxiv:2607.04037 — "Reward-Gated On-Policy Distillation"

**Relevance: ★★ — FUTURE CONSIDERATION (SFT quality filtering)**

**Key finding**: Standard teacher KL distillation applied uniformly causes the teacher to "pull" the student away from correct student continuations that the teacher would not have generated — effectively unlearning reward-certified good behaviors. Gating the distillation signal by reward (only apply KL where reward is low) prevents this failure mode.

**Connection to our pipeline**: Our SFT phase applies cross-entropy on ALL teacher tokens, regardless of whether the student would have produced a better solution. Reward-gated SFT would apply the teacher loss only on tasks where the student (small model) fails, and suppress it where the student already succeeds — concentrating teacher signal on the genuine difficulty band. This is complementary to EXP-139 (disagreement curriculum for GRPO) but acts at the SFT stage.

**Action**: Not queued today (at 2-experiment cap). Record for post-AAAI iteration: implement `sft_gate_by_student_pass=True` (filter traces to small_pass=False before SFT gradient). Pair with EXP-139 (disagreement curriculum GRPO) as a full "reward-gated distillation pipeline."
