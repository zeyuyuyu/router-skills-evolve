## Headline Findings (Top 5 Most Informative This Week)

No A800 experiments ran. Findings are new data from existing experiments and high-fidelity arxiv signals.

### 1. Non-Monotonic Cycle 1 Skills Arm Dip (65.85%) — New Data from grpo_info.json
**Why informative:** The per-cycle skills arm data (now complete in Table 9) shows the skills arm
*decreased* from 70.73% (cycle 0) to **65.85% (cycle 1)** (−4.88pp), then recovered to 73.17%
(cycle 2) and 75.61% (cycle 3). Critically, cycle 1 has the *lowest* ACR (47.6%) — meaning
more non-collapsed groups and more potential GRPO gradient — yet the worst skills arm.
This anti-correlation directly challenges the simple "fix ACR → fix Full arm" hypothesis.
It suggests a second failure mode: GRPO may be degrading SFT gains even when it produces
non-zero gradient (Hypothesis D). This is the most significant new analytical finding this week.
**Signal-to-noise:** Clean — 4 data points, full per-cycle ACR series for comparison.
**Control:** Router arm is stable (90.24%–93.90%), so the dip is specific to the skills arm.

### 2. Hypothesis E: Within-Cycle Rise-and-Collapse (arxiv:2606.21090)
**Why informative:** Pass@1 peaks within tens of gradient steps of each GRPO run and
collapses within the same run; KL constraints and EWC do not prevent it. If our cycle adapters
are saved at the final (collapsed) checkpoint, Full = Router is entirely explained without
any algorithmic change needed. EXP-132 (~3h GPU) is the highest-priority experiment in the
queue before the AAAI deadline: if confirmed, switching to best-epoch checkpointing could
recover 3–5pp on the full arm with zero other modifications.
**Effect size:** +3–5pp pass@1 predicted for best-epoch vs final checkpoint.

### 3. Hypothesis D: GRPO Forgets SFT Gains (CPO, arxiv:2607.04364)
**Why informative:** CPO proves RL fine-tuning forgets across diverse sequential tasks
(−13.7% forgetting on Qwen3-VL-8B). In our pipeline: GRPO gradients on hard-task groups
(advantage ≠ 0) may inadvertently degrade easy-task weights from Phase 3a SFT. Since
easy-task groups produce all-pass rollouts (advantage = 0), they receive zero GRPO gradient
and no weight protection. Consistent with the cycle-1 dip (lowest ACR = most hard-task
GRPO gradient = most potential forgetting of easy-task SFT gains). EXP-130 (CPO-PMP:
L2-SP + behavioral KL anchor against the SFT checkpoint) directly tests this.
**Effect size predicted:** Full arm 92.68% → ≥94% if Hypothesis D is the dominant mechanism.

### 4. SCCA: Self-Conditioned Credit Assignment (arxiv:2606.18810)
**Why informative:** Targets the 47.6% of non-ACR groups that carry ALL GRPO learning signal.
Current GRPO assigns uniform token-level credit within each winning rollout, including
stylistically coincidental "tail tokens." SCCA locates the first divergence token between
each failed rollout and its nearest successful rollout and applies distance-decayed credit.
Results: +3.1 pass@1 HumanEval, +2.7 MBPP on a 7B model. Consistent across benchmarks.
**Significance:** Even if ACR fixes (AVSPO, EDGE-GRPO, Dr.GRPO) succeed, 47.6% of non-ACR
groups still provide imprecise gradient — SCCA improves signal quality in all of them.

### 5. SC-SDPO: Pass-Rate Weighted SFT (arxiv:2605.27765)
**Why informative:** Skills arm is 20.7pp below always-large (75.61% vs 96.34%). Phase 3a
SFT uses uniform task weighting, so easy tasks (teacher trace trivial) and hard tasks
(teacher trace = noise) crowd out informative intermediate tasks. SC-SDPO weights each
task's SFT loss by ŵ = √[p̂(1−p̂)] — restoring the GRPO implicit p(1−p) curriculum to
SFT. Implementation: ~10 lines to traces_to_sft.py and train_small_model.py. Predicted
+2.4pp skills arm. Zero GPU overhead; orthogonal to all GRPO fixes queued.
**Ratio of predicted gain to implementation cost:** Highest in the SFT track this week.
