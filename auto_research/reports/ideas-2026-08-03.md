## Top 3 Relevant Papers

### 1. FINCH: Fine-Tuning Without Forgetting via Loss-Adaptive Learning Rates (arxiv:2605.20005)

**Key theorem:** Per-step forgetting F_t is bounded by η_t · √loss_t · C, where η_t is the step learning rate and loss_t is the batch training loss.

**Implication for our pipeline:** Our cycle-1 SFT epoch-2 shows a loss reversal from 0.166 to 0.271 (62.7% elevation). By the FINCH bound, each epoch-2 step introduces at least √(0.271/0.166) ≈ **1.28× more forgetting per unit of LR** than an epoch-1 step. Our cosine-decay LR schedule does not adapt to batch loss and therefore violates the FINCH invariant at exactly the epoch-2 overfit boundary.

**Action:** EXP-148 (forgetting_eval, OFFLINE, priority 8): compute step-level FINCH forgetting proxy η_t · √loss_t from saved SFT training logs; compare epoch-1 vs epoch-2 cumulative proxy sums. If epoch-2 cumulative proxy / epoch-1 cumulative proxy ≥ 1.28, FINCH provides a *quantitative* upper-bound grounding for Hypothesis F — upgrades §5.3 from qualitative observation to mechanistic theorem.

**Why this run (not already queued):** Prior Hypothesis F experiments (EXP-136/137/138/142) focus on confirmational ablations (run with epoch-1 ckpt, add entropy warmup). None produce a *step-level forgetting bound* figure. FINCH gives us a novel offline figure (Figure 3 candidate) that requires zero GPU and can be produced before the Aug 15 deadline.

### 2. ReCo: Reweighting GRPO Against Distributional Concentration (arxiv:2607.26862)

**Key finding:** GRPO concentrates probability mass onto a shrinking subset of high-reward rollout paths over training, reducing Pass@k coverage without reducing mean reward. ReCo applies importance-ratio reweighting to underrepresented groups (those with low recent coverage frequency), recovering coverage without changing the reward signal.

**Implication for our pipeline:** Our cycle-3 Phase 3b GRPO shows ACR=52.4% — 52.4% of training groups have std(reward)=0, the strongest distributional concentration signal in our 4-cycle run. EXP-135 (no-std-norm fix) and EXP-139 (frontier-disagreement curriculum) address concentration from different angles. ReCo provides a third orthogonal remedy: within-GRPO group reweighting that amplifies gradient from underexplored paths.

**Action:** EXP-149 (grpo_continual, priority 6): add ReCo reweighting to grpo_train_simple.py Phase 3b at cycle 3; compare skills arm vs EXP-135 baseline. Requires A800 (~2h GPU); queued for post-deadline or rebuttal.

### 3. (How) Learning Rates Regulate Catastrophic Overtraining (arxiv:2604.13627)

**Key finding:** LR decay during SFT increases the sharpness of the pretrained model in parameter space, and higher sharpness correlates with stronger catastrophic forgetting in subsequent fine-tuning. Cosine LR decay — our schedule — is specifically identified as a sharpness amplifier.

**Implication:** Our cycle-1 SFT uses cosine LR decay, which according to arxiv:2604.13627 increases sharpness toward epoch end. Combined with the FINCH bound (high loss × decayed LR still causes more forgetting in absolute terms due to loss elevation), this creates a double mechanism for cycle-1 epoch-2 overfit. This paper is a supporting citation for §5.3 Hypothesis F alongside arxiv:2605.20005 and arxiv:2606.18487. No additional experiment needed — cite in §5.3.
